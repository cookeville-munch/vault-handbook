# Evaluation Tools & Templates

## Practical Skills Assessment Rubric

### Pre-Scene Negotiation (20 minutes observed)

| Criterion | 1 - Insufficient | 2 - Developing | 3 - Competent | 4 - Proficient | 5 - Exemplary |
|-----------|------------------|----------------|---------------|----------------|---------------|
| **Consent Language** | Uses vague/assumptive language ("you're okay with this, right?") | Mixes specific and vague language | Uses affirmative consent consistently | Models nuanced consent (checks capacity, power dynamics) | Teaches consent skills through demonstration |
| **Boundary Coverage** | Misses 3+ required elements (limits, safewords, aftercare, health, triggers) | Misses 1-2 required elements | Covers all required elements thoroughly | Anticipates unstated boundaries, explores gray areas | Facilitates partner's boundary discovery |
| **Risk Discussion** | No risk discussion or dismissive of risks | Mentions risks generically | Discusses specific risks for planned activities | Collaborative risk mitigation planning | Integrates medical/psychological risk factors |
| **Communication Plan** | No mid-scene communication plan | Basic safeword only | Safewords + check-in schedule + non-verbal signals | Dynamic communication adapting to state changes | Creates shared language for subtle state shifts |
| **Aftercare Negotiation** | No aftercare discussion | Generic aftercare mention | Specific aftercare preferences for each participant | Contingency aftercare for different scene outcomes | Co-creates aftercare as integral to scene design |

### Scene Execution (45 minutes observed)

| Criterion | 1 - Insufficient | 2 - Developing | 3 - Competent | 4 - Proficient | 5 - Exemplary |
|-----------|------------------|----------------|---------------|----------------|---------------|
| **Safety Protocol Adherence** | Violates negotiated protocols or ignores distress signals | Inconsistent protocol adherence | Follows all negotiated protocols consistently | Proactively enhances safety beyond negotiated minimum | Innovates safety practices adoptable by others |
| **Technique Control** | Uncontrolled force, poor target accuracy, unsafe body mechanics | Basic control with occasional lapses | Consistent control, accurate targeting, safe mechanics | Precision control with intentional variation | Masterful control serving artistic/emotional intent |
| **State Monitoring** | No observable monitoring of partner state | Occasional check-ins only | Regular verbal/non-verbal check-ins per plan | Continuous attunement, adjusts in real-time | Anticipates state shifts before partner signals |
| **Adaptability** | Rigidly follows plan despite partner feedback | Minor adjustments only | Adapts intensity/pacing/activities per partner feedback | Creative adaptation enhancing experience | Transforms challenges into deeper connection |
| **Equipment Management** | Unsafe equipment use or neglected inspection | Basic inspection, functional use | Thorough inspection, skillful use, proper transitions | Optimizes equipment for partner's body/response | Improvises safely with available resources |

### Aftercare & Debrief (25 minutes observed)

| Criterion | 1 - Insufficient | 2 - Developing | 3 - Competent | 4 - Proficient | 5 - Exemplary |
|-----------|------------------|----------------|---------------|----------------|---------------|
| **Physical Aftercare** | No aftercare provided | Minimal/generic aftercare | Appropriate aftercare per negotiation | Anticipates unspoken physical needs | Creates restorative physical experience |
| **Emotional Presence** | Disengaged or distracted | Physically present, emotionally distant | Attentive, responsive to emotional cues | Creates safe container for emotional processing | Facilitates partner's emotional integration |
| **Debrief Quality** | No debrief or superficial | Covers basic "how was it" | Structured reflection on experience & learning | Connects experience to growth patterns | Co-creates meaning, plans future growth |
| **Follow-Up Plan** | No follow-up discussed | Vague "check in later" | Specific follow-up timeline/method | Proactive check-in scheduling | Builds ongoing care relationship |

## Written Knowledge Assessment Sample Questions

### Anatomy & Physiology (20 questions)

**Multiple Choice:**
1. The brachial plexus is most vulnerable to compression during which bondage position?
   A) Box tie with arms parallel
   B) Strappado with wrists behind back
   C) Hands-free suspension
   D) Frog tie with ankle-to-thigh

2. Impact to the lower back (kidney area) risks damage to which structures?
   A) Floating ribs only
   B) Kidneys, ureters, and quadratus lumborum
   C) Spinal cord and lumbar plexus
   D) Sciatic nerve and gluteal muscles

**Short Answer:**
3. Describe the physiological cascade during subspace, including at least three neurotransmitters/hormones involved and their typical temporal sequence.

4. Explain why nerve compression injuries may not be immediately apparent during rope bondage, and describe the earliest detectable signs.

### Risk Assessment & Emergency Procedures (15 questions)

**Case Analysis:**
5. During a suspension scene, the bottom reports "tingling in pinky finger and outer hand" 12 minutes into suspension. The top observes good capillary refill in fingertips. 
   a) What nerve is likely affected?
   b) What immediate action is required?
   c) What follow-up assessment should occur over the next 24 hours?

**Short Answer:**
6. List the contents of a kink-specific emergency kit and explain the indication for each item.

### Consent Law & Community Standards (15 questions)

**Multiple Choice:**
7. In jurisdictions where "consent is not a defense to assault," which factor most influences prosecution decisions in kink contexts?
   A) Whether safewords were used
   B) Whether serious bodily injury occurred
   C) Whether activities were pre-negotiated in writing
   D) Whether participants are in a romantic relationship

**Scenario Analysis:**
8. A community member reports that during a scene, their partner continued for 30 seconds after they said "wait, wait" (not their negotiated safeword). The partner claims they thought it was part of the roleplay.
   a) Analyze this using program consent principles
   b) What community response aligns with restorative justice principles?
   c) What prevention measures should the community implement?

### Equipment Physics & Materials (15 questions)

**Calculations:**
9. A 3mm jute rope rated at 180kg breaking strength is used for a 70kg person in a chest harness. What is the safety factor? If the same rope is used for a 100kg person in a single-column tie taking full body weight dynamically (2x safety factor recommended), is this acceptable?

**Material Science:**
10. Explain why nylon rope is generally unsuitable for suspension bondage compared to natural fibers, referencing elongation properties and heat generation.

### Neurobiology (15 questions)

**Short Answer:**
11. Differentiate between subspace, topspace, and flow state in terms of autonomic nervous system activation, prefrontal cortex activity, and subjective experience.

12. A participant reports experiencing "drop" 36 hours after an intense scene, characterized by depression, fatigue, and shame. Explain the neurobiological mechanisms and outline evidence-based interventions.

### Integration Case Studies (20 questions)

**Long-form Analysis (choose 2 of 4):**
13. Design a 6-month progression plan for a newcomer interested in rope suspension, including: prerequisite skills, assessment milestones, equipment acquisition, mentorship structure, and risk mitigation at each stage.

14. A long-term D/s couple wants to explore consensual non-consent (CNC). Outline the negotiation framework, safety architecture, and ongoing consent mechanisms you would teach them, addressing the unique ethical complexities of CNC.
