## Boundary Communication Frameworks

### Basic Boundary Template
"I'm interested in exploring [activity]. My hard limits are: [list]. My soft limits are: [list with conditions]. I need [specific aftercare]. How does that sound for you?"

### Audio Consent Template (for visually impaired)
**Structure**: 
1. Greeting & Context Setting
2. Activity Description (clear, sensory-focused)
3. Limit Declaration (hard/soft with specifics)
4. Safety Requirements (signals, equipment, monitoring)
5. Aftercare Needs (physical, emotional, temporal)
6. Partner Invitation for Reciprocal Sharing
7. Confirmation Check ("Does this work for you?")

### Visual Consent Template (for hearing impaired)
**Components**:
- **Activity Cards**: Picture-based activity options with consent check boxes
- **Limit Indicators**: Color-coded system (red=hard limit, yellow=soft with conditions, green=interested)
- **Aftercare Menu**: Visual options for physical/emotional care
- **Signal Cards**: Pre-made cards for check-ins (thumbs up/down, pause, stop)
- **Communication Board**: Pre-written phrases for common negotiation points

### Emoji Code System (for neurodivergent or text-based)
```
✅ = Enthusiastic yes, let's proceed
➖ = Maybe/slow down, need more info or check-in
❌ = Hard stop, not interested
⚠️ = Soft limit with conditions (explain in text)
💬 = Want to talk more about this
🛑 = Emergency stop, immediate pause needed
❤️ = Aftercare needed or being provided
⏸️ = Pause/check-in requested
🔄 = Willing to try with modifications
```

### Progressive Disclosure Template
**Stage 1: Basic Framework**
- Activity type: [impact/restraint/sensation/etc.]
- Intensity level: [low/medium/high]
- Duration estimate: [time frame]

**Stage 2: Details (if Stage 1 agreed upon)**
- Specific implements/actions
- Body areas involved/targeted
- Sensory qualities sought/avoided
- Power dynamic elements (if any)

**Stage 3: Safety & Aftercare**
- Required safety equipment
- Check-in frequency/signals
- Aftercare preferences & needs
- Emergency protocols

### Trauma-Informed Consent Template
**Principles**: Predictability, Choice, Collaboration, Empowerment, Safety
**Structure**:
1. **Safety First**: "Let's make sure we both feel safe before we begin"
2. **Options Presentation**: "Here are a few ways we could approach this..."
3. **Slow Pace**: "We can take as much time as you need at each step"
4. **Check-In Protocol**: "I'll check in with you every [time/action], and you can stop anytime"
5. **Aftercare Planning**: "What kind of care do you want afterward?"
6. **Ongoing Consent**: "Remember, you can change your mind at any point"

### Power Exchange Negotiation Framework
**For Dominant-Leaning Partners**:
- "I would like to take the lead in [specific ways]. My limits are [list]. I need [specific assurances] from you."
- "How would you feel about me [specific action]?"

**For Submissive-Leaning Partners**:
- "I'm interested in exploring [specific surrender]. My hard limits are [list]. I need [specific reassurances]."
- "What boundaries do you need to feel comfortable taking the lead?"

**For Switch/Negotiated Dynamics**:
- "Let's map out who leads when and what that looks like."
- "We can switch roles based on [criteria] or [time intervals]."

### Group/Play Party Negotiation Template
**Pre-Event**:
- Share general interests/limits with organizers if requested
- Review party-specific rules and consent culture
- Establish personal check-in buddy if attending alone

**During Event**:
- Use party's designated consent system (colored bracelets, specific safewords)
- Clearly communicate limits to potential partners before play
- Utilize designated quiet spaces for negotiation if needed
- Respect others' negotiation space and privacy

**Post-Play**:
- Debrief with play partner using aftercare preferences
- Check-in with buddy or community support if needed
- Report any concerns to party safety team via established channels

### Digital/Remote Negotiation Template
**Video Call Specifics**:
- Verify identity through pre-agreated method
- Ensure private, secure connection
- Test audio/video clarity before beginning
- Establish backup communication method (text/phone)

**Text/Chat Specifics**:
- Use clear, explicit language (no sarcasm/ambiguity)
- Confirm receipt and understanding of important points
- Establish response time expectations for check-ins
- Save/negotiate consent logs with mutual agreement

**Teledildonics/App-Controlled Specifics**:
- Confirm app connection and functionality test
- Establish maximum intensity limits in advance
- Agree on emergency stop procedures within app/device
- Plan for latency/disconnection scenarios

## Special Population Adaptations

### For Cognitive Disabilities
- **Concrete Language**: Avoid metaphors, use literal descriptions
- **Visual Supports**: Pair words with pictures/icons
- **Repetition**: Review key points multiple times
- **Choice Limitation**: Offer 2-3 clear options rather than open-ended
- **Consistency**: Use same phrases/terms throughout negotiation

### For Chronic Pain/Fatigue
- **Energy Budgeting**: Discuss spoons/energy levels beforehand
- **Position Flexibility**: Offer multiple positioning options
- **Pain Monitoring**: Establish clear pain check-in protocols
- **Session Length**: Negotiate based on current capacity, not ideals
- **Heat/Cold Options**: Have available for localized relief

### For Neurodivergent Partners
- **Predictability**: Clear agenda, advance notice of changes
- **Sensory Information**: Describe expected sensations (texture, temperature, sound)
- **Literal Interpretation**: Avoid idioms, sarcasm, implied meanings
- **Processing Time**: Allow ample time to respond to questions
- **Alternative Communication**: Accept written/drawn responses if preferred

### For Elder Practitioners
- **Pacing Considerations**: Allow for slower negotiation and execution
- **Health Check-Ins**: Discuss cardiovascular, joint, balance considerations
- **Comfort Prioritization**: Emphasize comfort over aesthetic or intensity
- **Memory Aids**: Offer written summaries of agreements
- **Physical Support**: Discuss need for assistance with positioning/mobility

## Practice Exercises

### Solo Practice
1. Write out your ideal scene using the basic boundary template
2. Identify 3 hard limits and phrase them clearly
3. Create a soft limit with specific conditions
4. Practice saying your aftercare needs aloud
5. Record yourself negotiating and listen for judgmental language

### Partner Practice
1. Exchange boundary templates without discussion, then discuss
2. Practice the audio consent template with eyes closed
3. Use the emoji code system for a full negotiation via text only
4. Negotiate a scene using only the visual consent template (no speech)
5. Practice trauma-informed approach with a partner who shares a trigger scenario

### Group Practice
1. Negotiate a scene in a trio where all must agree
2. Practice using a community party's consent system (colors, safewords)
3. Role-play intervening when someone appears uncomfortable during negotiation
4. Create a group consent agreement for a workshop or class setting

## Quick Reference: Phrases to Avoid & Alternatives

### Avoid: Assumptive Language
- Instead of: "You're okay with this, right?"
- Try: "How do you feel about trying this?"

### Avoid: Pressure/Persuasion
- Instead of: "Come on, just try it a little..."
- Try: "No pressure at all - we can stick with what you know you like"

### Avoid: Judgment/Shame
- Instead of: "That's weird/creepy"
- Try: "That's not my thing, but I'm glad you know what you like"

### Avoid: Vagueness
- Instead of: "Let's see what happens"
- Try: "Let's agree on [specific action] for [specific time] with [specific check-in]"

### Avoid: Ignoring Nonverbal Cues
- Instead of: Continuing despite hesitation
- Try: "You seem unsure - want to talk more about this?"
